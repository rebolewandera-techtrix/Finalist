ChainGrade / How It Works

Three steps. One permanent record.

Each simulation below runs in your browser - click through to see exactly what happens to your submission at every stage of the ChainGrade pipeline.

Stage 01

Connect your wallet.
Claim your identity.

Think of your wallet like a personal stamp - unique to you and impossible to copy. When you connect it to ChainGrade, your public address becomes the permanent root of every submission you ever make. No email, no username - your cryptographic key is the record.

Under the hood: your wallet signs a challenge with its private key. The resulting public address - derived from that signature - becomes your identity anchor on the Polygon Amoy ledger. The private key stays on your device at all times. Only the signed assertion hits the chain.

Stage 02

Upload your project.
Lock its fingerprint to the chain.

When you submit a file, ChainGrade generates a unique fingerprint of it and records that fingerprint on the blockchain. The actual file lives on IPFS - a global distributed storage network - so there's no single server that can lose or delete it. Even changing one character in your file produces a completely different fingerprint, so any tampering is detectable on the spot.

IPFS assigns every file a content-addressed CID - the hash of the file's raw bytes. That CID is what gets written on-chain via a recordProject() smart contract call on Polygon Amoy. The ledger holds the proof; IPFS holds the payload. Neither depends on a central server staying online or cooperative.

Stage 03

Faculty review.
On-chain approval trail.

Verification isn't a rubber stamp from one person. Faculty reviewers each sign off using their own wallet, and every signature is stored on-chain separately. You set the threshold - 2-of-3, 3-of-5, any combination. Once the threshold is met, the record is permanently marked as verified. The full approval history is public: who signed, when, and exactly what they signed.

Faculty hold Soul-Bound Tokens (SBTs) - non-transferable credentials issued by the contract admin that authorise their approval signature. The smart contract records each approver's wallet address, block timestamp, and the exact content hash they approved. Revocations are separate on-chain events with their own audit trail - nothing is silently edited.
